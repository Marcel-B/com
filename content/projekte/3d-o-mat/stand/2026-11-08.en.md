A skeleton alone does not make a figure come alive. The test moves 3d-o-mat brought along so far are computed, and
walking in particular looked stiff on some figures. Now there is real motion: a library of 244 motion capture
recordings from two free collections, from walking in fifteen moods to boxing, salsa and cartwheels.

One click plays a recording on your own figure. Each motion is carried over to its skeleton: the bones are matched,
the rest poses aligned and the steps fitted to the length of the legs. Speed, start and end can be adjusted, and the
figure can stay in place or be mirrored. Up to twelve motions go into one GLB as animations that Godot or Blender read
directly.

The recordings come from the CMU Motion Capture Database, which is free for commercial use too, and from a Bandai Namco
Research dataset that may only be used non-commercially. The page marks those recordings and warns when they are part of
a selection. It has been tried with real figures; whether the animations look the same in Godot as in the browser is
still to be seen.

The second step followed the same day. When a recording is carried over to a different figure, the feet slide a little
because the legs have a different length. Now 3d-o-mat looks for the moments in each recording when a foot is on the
ground and holds it there. The legs adapt, and no foot sinks below the floor. A loop blends the end smoothly into the
start, so a walk cycle repeats without a jump. On sturdy figures the arms can be turned a little away from the body, so
the hands do not end up in the belly. For Godot, the forward movement can come as its own track (root motion). This has
been tried in the browser, not yet in Godot. Along the way it turned out that the skeleton does have fingers (two to five chains per hand, depending on the figure)
but they carried no names, which is why the fist sliders did nothing on some figures. Now the fingers get their names,
on new skeletons as on old ones, and every recording from the library can run with closed hands. Next, the skeleton's
joints should be movable by hand where they do not quite fit.
