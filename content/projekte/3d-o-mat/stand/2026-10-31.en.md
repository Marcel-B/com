A figure in a T-pose is handy for animation, but not a nice model for the shelf. So 3d-o-mat is getting a way to the
3D printer: you can put the figure into a pose, with ready-made poses, with sliders for every bone or with a sentence
like “standing relaxed, right hand waving” that Gemma turns into joint angles. Then the pose is baked into the mesh,
everything is merged into one watertight body and placed on a base. The result is a 3MF file for common slicers.

Only the shape is printed: whatever the model only painted into its texture, such as eyes or patterns, is missing from
a single-colour print. The print file and the poses have been tried on a figure made in 3d-o-mat. Gemma gets a
described pose only roughly right; the sliders let you fine-tune it afterwards. If the skeleton has fingers, the figure
can also make fists.
